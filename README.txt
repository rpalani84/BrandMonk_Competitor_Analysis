COMPETITOR ANALYSIS AGENT
Customer Guide

1. PURPOSE
This application helps users organize competitor research, review available evidence, compare relevant businesses, and inspect website audit results. Findings should be interpreted alongside their evidence and limitations.

2. WHAT IS A COMPETITOR?
A competitor is a business that competes for similar customers, needs, budgets, or business opportunities. Similarity alone does not prove that two companies compete directly.

3. TYPES OF COMPETITORS
Direct competitor: Offers similar products or services to a similar target audience.
Indirect competitor: Meets a similar customer need through a different product, service, or approach.
Potential competitor: May compete in the future, but the overlap needs further verification.
Needs verification: Available evidence is insufficient to make a confident classification.
These definitions are a guide for interpreting research. A classification should only be treated as confirmed when supported by evidence.

4. UNDERSTANDING THE SCORES
Website audit score: Summarizes the technical website checks performed by the application. Interpret it according to the checks and scoring rules used.
Competitive strength: Represents competitive performance only when the application has sufficient relevant evidence and an applicable scoring method.
Evidence coverage: Indicates how much of the required competitive information is available or supported. Check the dashboard's definition of coverage when interpreting a percentage.

IMPORTANT: A high website audit score does not mean a company is the strongest competitor, the market leader, or the best service provider. A technically healthy website and a strong competitive position are different things.

5. MISSING DATA AND EVIDENCE
A blank or unavailable score means that a reliable score may not be available; it must not automatically be interpreted as zero.
Low evidence coverage means that conclusions may be incomplete. Do not treat missing information as proof that a company does not offer a service.
Review the available sources, source dates, and supporting details before relying on a finding. Where evidence is conflicting or insufficient, mark the conclusion for further verification.

6. HOW TO USE THE RESULTS
• Confirm that the businesses being compared serve relevant customer groups and markets.
• Compare similar products, services, course offerings, locations, and customer needs where applicable.
• Review source evidence and evidence coverage before drawing conclusions.
• Use website audit results to assess the technical checks performed, not to rank competitors overall.
• Consider the research date because business offerings and websites can change.
• Verify important findings independently before making commercial or strategic decisions.

7. LIMITATIONS
Results depend on the sources collected, the information available at the time of research, the checks performed, and the scoring rules configured in the application.
Automated research or website checks may not capture every business offering, customer segment, market condition, or technical issue. A successful website response does not establish business quality or market success.
Do not infer a market leader, definitive ranking, or competitive advantage from incomplete evidence or website audit scores alone.

8. RESPONSIBLE INTERPRETATION
This application is a research and decision-support tool. Its results are not a guarantee of market position or future business performance. Users should review supporting evidence and apply their own business judgment.
